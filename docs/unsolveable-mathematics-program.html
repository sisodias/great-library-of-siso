UNSOLVEABLE Mathematics is the proposed SISO research programme for discovering important open mathematical questions, preserving their exact statements and histories, mapping rewards and active communities, learning from earlier breakthroughs, and running AI-assisted campaigns whose claims survive novelty review, statement-fidelity review, formal verification, independent replay, and human mathematical scrutiny.

Placement decision
This begins as an authored Research module beside the existing Frontier Questions model and question-driven research architecture. It is not a loose maths folder and it is not a second registry. A selected mathematical problem becomes a research-question Work; a verified accepted solution or disproof becomes an immutable Answer Release; the current portfolio is selected through a Snapshot. External catalogues enter through evidence-led Source Inventories and their payloads remain with their rightful owners.

“Unsolvable” is not one status

The public name is intentionally ambitious, but the data must be precise. The programme must never collapse fundamentally different states into a single label.

Open / unsolved

No accepted proof or counterexample is known within the audited evidence universe.

Partially resolved

Important cases, bounds, reductions, or conditional forms are known, while the canonical question remains open.

Claimed / disputed

A proposed resolution exists but novelty, correctness, statement match, or community acceptance remains unsettled.

Independent

The statement is proved independent of a named axiom system, so “solve it” requires changing or clarifying foundations.

Undecidable

No algorithm can decide every instance in the stated class; individual instances may still be resolvable.

Impossible / non-existent

The mathematical answer is a theorem that an object, algorithm, construction, or bound cannot exist.

Solved, informally checked

A mathematical proof is accepted but no machine-checked formalisation is selected.

Formally verified

An exact formal statement has a kernel-checked proof under an explicit axiom and dependency set.

Resolved and accepted

Correctness, novelty, canonical statement, publication, and relevant community or prize procedures are complete.

Honesty rule: “No solution found” means only that a declared search and evidence universe found none. It is not proof that a problem remains open. Every status needs a source, observation date, confidence, and refresh trigger.

What OpenAI’s ten-proofs contributes

The reusable idea is not “ask a model for ten answers.” It is an evidence and verification spine connecting a mathematical claim to an exact machine-checkable declaration.

Named result declarations

Every main result is tied to an exact Lean declaration and source file, rather than only a prose title.

Explicit proof debt

The manifest records zero sorry placeholders and distinguishes placeholders inside definitions.

Axiom inventory

Permitted axioms are listed per result, making foundational dependence inspectable.

Source provenance

The project records paper identity, date, authorship, prior work, review state, and formalisation scope.

Independent replay

Comparator challenge configurations separate the trusted statement from the candidate solution and replay exported proof terms through independent checking machinery.

Reasoning history

The walkthroughs preserve obstacles, dead ends, perspective changes, and decisive pivots instead of exposing only polished final proofs.

The six claims that must remain separate

ClaimQuestion it answersEvidence required
Kernel correctnessDoes the formal proof term type-check under the declared foundations?Pinned toolchain, zero placeholders, dependency and axiom audit, reproducible build.
Statement fidelityIs the formal theorem exactly the intended mathematical claim?Bilingual statement map, expert review, quantified assumptions and exclusions, challenge tests.
NoveltyWas this result already known, implied, published, or independently discovered?Literature search, citation graph, timestamped provenance, domain-expert review.
IndependenceDid the claimed agent or method produce the decisive work without hidden solution leakage?Data and prompt lineage, contamination audit, prior-work disclosure, reproducible campaign receipts.
Mathematical acceptanceDo qualified mathematicians accept the argument and its significance?Human review, publication, correction history, community response.
Prize eligibilityDoes a solution satisfy the administrator’s legal and procedural rules?Current official rules, qualifying publication, time periods, acceptance and attribution requirements.

Non-negotiable: a Lean file can be perfectly correct while proving the wrong theorem, a weaker theorem, a theorem with stronger hidden assumptions, or a theorem that was already known. Formal verification closes one failure channel; it does not close them all.

The verification ladder

  1. Discovery receipt

    A candidate problem, prize, claim, project, or source has been found and classified. No open-status claim is made yet.

  2. Canonical statement lock

    Original or institutionally authoritative sources have been read; aliases, variants, quantifiers, assumptions, and exclusions are explicit.

  3. Status and novelty audit

    Recent literature, references, trackers, and experts have been checked. Competing claims and search limits are recorded.

  4. Formal statement fidelity

    The informal and formal statements are mapped in both directions. Test cases expose vacuous, strengthened, weakened, or mistranslated forms.

  5. Candidate mathematical result

    A proof, disproof, improved bound, reduction, algorithm, or computational certificate exists with complete derivation and provenance.

  6. Primary formal verification

    The proof builds with a pinned toolchain, explicit dependencies and axioms, zero unapproved placeholders, and reproducible commands.

  7. Independent proof audit

    A separated trusted statement and checker replay the result, ideally through an independent kernel, exporter, or certificate verifier.

  8. Adversarial mathematical review

    Domain experts attack assumptions, edge cases, novelty, significance, and statement match. Criticism and corrections remain public where safe.

  9. Publication and institutional gates

    The relevant journal, community, benchmark, or prize process has completed its own required procedure and waiting periods.

  10. Immutable Answer Release

    The Great Library publishes the exact accepted answer, evidence universe, grade, limitations, formal artifacts, and triggers for correction or successor work.

The intelligence system

These are typed responsibilities. One agent may perform several roles, but no role should silently certify its own output.

Frontier Scout

Discovers problems, prizes, new claims, benchmark lists, and source changes.

Identity Resolver

Deduplicates aliases and variants; binds each candidate to canonical sources and exact scope.

Mathematical Historian

Builds the chronology of partial results, failed approaches, key tools, and community structure.

Novelty Auditor

Searches prior literature and equivalent formulations; tries to prove the “new” result is already known.

Formalisation Architect

Maps prerequisites, chooses prover and representations, and defines statement-fidelity tests.

Proof Researcher

Develops arguments, reductions, constructions, bounds, algorithms, and experiments.

Counterexample Hunter

Attacks conjectures and candidate lemmas through adversarial search, computation, and edge cases.

Certificate Engineer

Turns computational work into compact, reproducible, independently checkable evidence.

Kernel Formalizer

Produces the machine-checked proof while tracking placeholders, axioms, imports, and toolchain state.

Comparator / Auditor

Checks exported terms or certificates against a separated trusted challenge and records the replay receipt.

Human Referee

Reviews mathematical meaning, novelty, attribution, significance, and any gap between formal and canonical statements.

Portfolio Governor

Allocates attention by value and learning, prevents duplicate campaigns, enforces stop conditions, and selects Releases.

How earlier communities solved hard problems

UNSOLVEABLE should preserve solution processes as reusable playbooks. A case study is not a hero biography; it is a causal map of representations, tools, coordination, dead ends, verification, and decisive insight.

CaseCoordination and proof mediumTransferable lesson
Polymath8 and bounded gapsOpen, timestamped collaboration rapidly improved numerical bounds after Zhang’s breakthrough while participants clarified and modularised the argument.Begin from a real foothold, expose work in public, split numerical optimisation from conceptual understanding, and let many contributors improve independent modules.
Polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa formalisationA large Lean collaboration formalised a newly announced modern combinatorics result in roughly three weeks.Real-time formalisation is viable when prerequisite libraries are strong, the proof decomposes cleanly, and coordination is explicit.
Liquid Tensor ExperimentA public challenge, blueprint, shared library work, and sustained Lean collaboration formally verified the main theorem over about eighteen months.For deep prerequisite-heavy mathematics, the blueprint and library-building programme are first-class deliverables, not overhead.
Flyspeck / Kepler conjectureA multi-year project used HOL Light and Isabelle to verify both conventional mathematics and computer-heavy components of a proof that had strained ordinary review.Long proofs need decomposition, independently checkable computational components, and auditing of the formalisation itself.
Boolean Pythagorean triplesCube-and-Conquer SAT solving resolved the problem and emitted a very large proof certificate for independent checking.Computation is mathematical evidence only when the search space, solver, certificate format, reconstruction, and checker are preserved.
OpenAI ten-proofsAn AI research campaign paired manuscripts and reasoning reconstructions with exact Lean declarations, zero-placeholder manifests, axiom inventories, and Comparator challenges.Machine discovery becomes durable only when narrative reasoning, formal proof, provenance, and independent replay are connected but separately assessed.

Case-study template

For every previous resolution record: canonical problem; state before breakthrough; people and organisations; prior bottleneck; decisive representation change; new lemma or tool; division of labour; computational resources; failed routes; proof medium; verification chain; time and cost; attribution; objections and corrections; transferable playbook; conditions under which the playbook would fail.

Portfolio selection: do not begin with fame alone

The six unsolved Millennium problems are essential north stars, but they are poor choices for the entire first attack portfolio. The programme should maximise both expected mathematical value and expected learning per unit of research effort.

DimensionPositive signalPenalty or risk
ImpactScientific importance, useful intermediate results, meaningful reward, broad dependency unlock.Prestige without a tractable subproblem or measurable feedback loop.
Formalisation readinessCanonical statement, existing formal declaration, strong library prerequisites, small fidelity gap.Decades of prerequisite formalisation before the first testable lemma.

Recommended pilot composition

North-star lane

The six open Millennium problems, captured as high-authority metadata and dependency maps—not represented as near-term autonomous targets.

Reward lane

The Beal Conjecture plus a small source-audited set of reward-bearing Erdős problems, especially candidates with existing formal statements.

Benchmark lane

The AIM 2026 AI-and-number-theory problems and Lean benchmarks, chosen because current AI limits and evaluation intent are explicit.

Formalisation lane

Open problems with canonical informal sources and existing Lean declarations, used to test statement-fidelity and prerequisite mapping before proof search.

Computation lane

One problem where finite search, SAT/SMT, verified numerics, or certificate construction can produce frequent falsifiable feedback.

Negative-control lane

Known solved, independent, or intentionally mistranslated fixtures that test whether the system invents novelty, confuses status, or accepts a proof of the wrong statement.

Campaign operating loop

  1. Lock the question. Resolve identity, exact statement, variants, foundations, reward rules, current status, and publication boundary.
  2. Build the dependency graph. Map definitions, equivalent formulations, prerequisite theories, formal-library coverage, partial results, and known obstructions.
  3. Reconstruct history. Record what was tried, why it failed, which approaches remain live, and where a representation change created progress in analogous problems.
  4. Pre-register hypotheses. State candidate routes, likely bottlenecks, falsifiers, measurable intermediate results, and stop conditions before generating large searches.
  5. Run diverse lanes. Proof search, counterexample search, computation, literature audit, representation discovery, and formalisation proceed independently enough to challenge one another.
  6. Preserve dead ends. Failed lemmas and counterexamples become structured learning capital; they are not deleted from the narrative.
  7. Formalise early enough. Use formalisation to reveal ambiguity and missing prerequisites, but do not force every speculative branch through a prover before it earns that cost.
  8. Separate solver and verifier. The system that generated a result does not control the trusted challenge, independent checker, novelty verdict, or final Answer Release.
  9. Escalate to humans. Engage appropriate mathematicians with a complete, respectful, source-grounded package—not mass unsolicited claims.
  10. Release or kill. Publish a verified result, a useful partial result, a benchmark/library improvement, or an explicit failed-campaign report with next triggers.

Governance and safety

  • Never announce that SISO or an agent has solved an open problem before canonical statement, novelty, correctness, independent verification, attribution, and publication boundaries are satisfied.
  • Never contact prize administrators with raw model output. Follow their current official rules; several explicitly reject direct submissions.
  • Credit original authors, prior partial results, formal-library contributors, proof engineers, computational infrastructure, reviewers, and independent rediscoveries.
  • Respect project intentions and student work. Collaboration intelligence is for coordination, not scooping.
  • Do not publish private correspondence, reviewer identities, licensed papers, proprietary databases, personal notes, credentials, machine-specific paths, or unpublished claims without permission.
  • Do not treat model confidence, repository popularity, citations, benchmark score, or a single successful verifier as mathematical consensus.
  • Preserve corrections. An invalidated Answer Release gets a successor or correction record; history is not rewritten.

Programme thesis: the durable advantage is not a model that occasionally guesses a proof. It is a living system that knows which questions exist, what they exactly mean, who has worked on them, what failed, what changed, what can be formalised now, which result is genuinely new, and which evidence is strong enough to become public mathematical memory.
